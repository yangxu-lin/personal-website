About

Yangxu Lin is currently a Research Assistant at the University of Oxford's Department of Social Policy and Intervention (DSPI), and holds an MSc in Economics for Development from Oxford's Department of Economics and the Department of International Development (ODID). Her research currently focuses on social interventions that improve child and adolescent education, health, and well-being in Africa and China, including cash transfer programmes and community health workers. She is also interested in firms and industrial policy, and in understanding how human capital investments translate into real economic gains.

At Oxford, she works with the Accelerate Research Hub and collaborates with UNICEF, the Gates Foundation, and NGOs and government ministries across Africa. She has contributed to several high-impact investment case studies, leading the costing analysis for scaling up programmes to eradicate child marriage in Ethiopia, contributing to a similar investment case in Northern Nigeria, and leading the quantitative modelling, return-on-investment analysis, and drafting for a WHO road safety investment case in Tunisia. She has also worked on the economic evaluation of a sugar-sweetened beverage tax in Thailand while serving with UNDP. She has strong expertise in causal inference, econometrics, evidence synthesis, and economic evaluation, and is committed to applying rigorous empirical methods to policy-relevant questions in economics, public policy, and international development.
